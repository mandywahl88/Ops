From Manual Follow-Ups to an Automated 60-Day Check-In Workflow

Designed by Mandy Wahl · AI-assisted process design and prototype

The business problem

Managing teams across multiple locations makes it easy for routine employee check-ins to fall through the cracks. A consistent 60-day cadence helps managers identify obstacles, offer support and create accountability without relying on memory or scattered notes.

The solution

I used conversational AI to help turn an operational need into a repeatable workflow: a check-in tracker with automatically calculated due dates, market-level organization, and a companion conversation guide. The original spreadsheet concept uses the formula =IF(C5="","",C5+60) to calculate the next check-in date.

This public demo reproduces the scheduling logic in a small interactive web prototype using fictional data.

My role and learning process

I defined the operational requirement, the 60-day service cadence, the fields managers needed and the expected follow-up behavior. I used AI as a collaborative design assistant to structure the tracker, explore formula logic and refine a practical management workflow.

The process reinforced how to translate ambiguous operations problems into measurable rules, review AI-generated work for usefulness and keep human judgment central to employee conversations.

How success could be measured

  • Percentage of check-ins completed within 60 days
  • Number of overdue conversations by market
  • Reduction in manual date tracking and missed follow-ups
  • Action items closed after check-in conversations

Scope: This is a workflow prototype and case study, not a claim of measured business impact or a deployed AI application.

Privacy and responsible use

The demonstration uses invented employees and sample markets. It does not include proprietary schedules, internal systems, employee performance records, or confidential company information. AI assisted with planning and workflow design; human review remains necessary for decisions affecting employees.
